# Shuttrix Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Shuttrix is a camera app for Android. The app sends no data to the
developer. Your photos and videos, and the date, camera and GPS tags in
them, stay on your phone. The banner in the gallery comes from Google
AdMob, which collects the data listed under "Ads".

## What the app stores, and where

- **Photos and videos** go to the camera folder of your phone, like
  photos from any camera app. They leave the phone only when you share
  them or another app you installed copies them.
- **Tags in each file** hold the capture date, the camera settings and,
  when location is on, the GPS point and altitude. They are written into
  the photo on the phone.
- **Settings** such as the language, the photo size and the dials stay in
  the app's storage on the phone.

## What the app does with phone permissions

- **Camera** takes the photos and videos you shoot.
- **Microphone** records the sound of your videos. The app records only
  while a video records.
- **Location** writes the GPS point into the photos and videos you take.
  The point stays in the file on your phone. Turn location off in Android
  Settings, Apps, Shuttrix, Permissions, and the app saves photos without
  it.
- **Photos and videos** lets the gallery show and edit the shots in the
  camera folder. You can take this back in Android Settings, Apps,
  Shuttrix, Permissions.
- **Install apps**, in the version from workbench-soft.com only, hands a
  newer Shuttrix to the Android installer, which asks you before it
  installs.

## On the phone only

- **QR codes and the subject** for Portrait are found by Google ML
  Kit on the phone. The camera picture doesn't leave the phone for this.
  Google Play services can download the ML Kit models.
- **Edits** such as straighten, crop and colour run on the phone and save
  a copy there.

## Network connections the app makes

- **Google AdMob**, to load the banner in the gallery. See "Ads" below.
- **Google Play**, to sell "Remove ads" and check that your Google
  account owns it. See "Remove ads" below.
- **GitHub**, in the version from workbench-soft.com only, to look for a
  newer version and download it. GitHub sees the request like any web
  request. The version from Google Play doesn't make this check.
- **The share sheet**, when you share a photo, sends it to the app you
  pick.

The app sends no photo, video or location to the developer.

## Ads

The gallery shows one banner from Google AdMob, under the grid of shots.
The camera, the photo viewer and Settings show no ads, and a gallery
opened over the lock screen shows none either.

- **What Google collects** the advertising ID of the phone, an
  approximate location from the IP address, how you interact with the
  ads, and crash and performance data from the ads code.
- **What for** to pick and measure ads, and to stop fraud. Google
  explains it at
  <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>.
- **Your choice** in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the app asks for
  consent the first time you open the gallery, before an ad loads.
  Settings, "Ad privacy choices" changes the answer. Elsewhere, Android
  Settings, Privacy, Ads resets or deletes the advertising ID.
- **No photo data** goes to Google. The ads code sees no photo, video or
  GPS tag.

## Remove ads

"Remove ads" is a one time purchase in Settings or above the gallery
banner. It turns the banner off on each phone signed in to the same
Google account.

- **Payment** Google Play takes the payment. The developer gets no card
  details, name or email from it.
- **Check** when you open the gallery, the app asks Google Play whether
  your account owns "Remove ads". The answer stays on the phone.

## Analytics, accounts

The app has no analytics of its own and no user accounts.

## Deleting your data

Uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android Settings, Apps,
Shuttrix, Storage. Either removes the settings. Your photos and videos
stay in the camera folder, and you delete them like any other photo.

## Children

The app is not meant for children, and it doesn't knowingly collect
data from them.
