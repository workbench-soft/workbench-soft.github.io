## A calendar of animals

The Chinese zodiac, called shengxiao, gives each year one of twelve animals in a fixed order: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig. The cycle repeats every twelve years. People born in a year are said to share some of its animal's character.

Unlike the Western zodiac, which follows the Sun through the months, the Chinese zodiac follows years. It is used across East and Southeast Asia, and millions of people celebrate the change of animal at the Lunar New Year.

## Where it comes from

The twelve animals match the twelve Earthly Branches, an old Chinese way of counting used for years, months, days and even hours. Ancient astronomers also tracked Jupiter, which takes about twelve years to circle the sky, and called it the year star. Over the centuries the branches took on animal names, and by the Han dynasty, about two thousand years ago, the animal cycle was well known.

## The Great Race

A favourite legend explains the order. The Jade Emperor invited all animals to a race across a river. The first twelve to arrive would each get a year.

1. The clever Rat rode on the Ox's back and jumped ashore first. The hard working Ox came second.
2. The Tiger fought the current and came third. The Rabbit hopped across on stones and a floating log.
3. The Dragon came fifth, having stopped to bring rain to a thirsty village. The Snake hid on the Horse's hoof and slipped ahead of it.
4. The Goat, the Monkey and the Rooster crossed on a raft together. The playful Dog stopped for a swim, and the Pig stopped for a meal and a nap.

The Cat, who had asked the Rat to wake it, slept through the race. Some say that is why cats chase rats. In Vietnam the Cat takes the Rabbit's place in the cycle.

## The five elements and the 60 year cycle

Each year also has one of five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. Each element lasts two years, and the full cycle of animals and elements together takes sixty years. So 1984 and 2044 are Wood Rat years, while 1996 is a Fire Rat and 2008 an Earth Rat. A quick rule uses the last digit of the year: 0 or 1 is Metal, 2 or 3 Water, 4 or 5 Wood, 6 or 7 Fire, 8 or 9 Earth.

Each animal also alternates between yin and yang. Rat, Tiger, Dragon, Horse, Monkey and Dog are yang. Ox, Rabbit, Snake, Goat, Rooster and Pig are yin.

## Friends and rivals

The animals form four groups of three that are said to get on well: Rat, Dragon and Monkey; Ox, Snake and Rooster; Tiger, Horse and Dog; Rabbit, Goat and Pig. Animals six years apart, such as Rat and Horse, are said to clash.

## More than the year

Traditional Chinese astrology, called Four Pillars or Bazi, uses the animals and elements of the year, month, day and hour of birth. The year animal is the best known pillar, but a full reading uses all four.
