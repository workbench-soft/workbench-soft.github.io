## The Snake at a glance

- **Recent years** 1965, 1977, 1989, 2001, 2013, 2025, and next 2037.
- **Order** sixth of the twelve animals.
- **Fixed element** Fire.
- **Polarity** yin.
- **Hour** 9 am to 11 am.
- **Best friends** the Ox and the Rooster, and the Monkey as a secret friend.
- **Clash** the Pig.

## The Snake in the Great Race

The Snake knew it could not outrun the Horse, so it quietly coiled around the Horse's hoof. As the Horse neared the finish, the Snake slid off and startled it, slipping in to take sixth place. The legend shows the Snake's patience and its clever, quiet way of reaching its goals.

In Chinese culture the Snake is sometimes called the little dragon. It stands for wisdom, mystery and renewal, since it sheds its skin and emerges fresh.

## The Snake's character

People born in a Snake year are often described as wise, calm and thoughtful. They think deeply, observe closely and rarely rush. Many are elegant and have a quiet, magnetic charm. They trust their intuition and often sense what others are hiding.

Snakes like to keep their plans to themselves. They prefer a few close friends to a big crowd. When they decide on a goal, they pursue it with steady focus.

## Strengths

- **Wisdom** Snakes think before they act and often see the bigger picture.
- **Intuition** they read people and situations well.
- **Composure** they stay calm in a crisis.
- **Elegance** they have style and good taste.

## Challenges

The Snake's privacy can come across as secrecy or coldness. It may hold grudges or become possessive. It can also take its time so long that chances slip away. Traditional advice encourages the Snake to share its thoughts and to trust others a little more.

## The Snake in love and friendship

In love the Snake is devoted, sensual and protective. It chooses partners carefully and wants a deep, loyal bond. It can be jealous if it feels insecure. Tradition pairs it best with the Ox and the Rooster, who share its steadiness, and with the Monkey, whose cleverness fascinates it.

## The Snake at work

Snakes do well in work that needs depth and strategy: research, science, philosophy, psychology, finance, law, writing and the arts. They like to work on their own terms and at their own pace.

## The five kinds of Snake

- **Wood Snake** 1965, 2025. Creative, kind and good at planning.
- **Fire Snake** 1977. Passionate, confident and outgoing.
- **Earth Snake** 1989. Calm, practical and loyal.
- **Metal Snake** 2001. Determined, clever and independent.
- **Water Snake** 2013. Intuitive, wise and adaptable.

## Snake years in the calendar

Snake years are often seen as times for reflection, study and careful planning. They follow the lively Dragon year with a calmer mood. The year begins at the Lunar New Year, which falls between late January and mid February.

## The Snake in myth

In Chinese myth, the goddess Nüwa, who made humans from clay, is often drawn with the body of a snake. The Legend of the White Snake, about a snake spirit who falls in love with a human, is one of China's most loved folk tales and has been retold in opera and film for centuries.
