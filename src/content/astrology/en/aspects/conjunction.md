## The conjunction in short

- **Angle** 0 degrees: two planets at the same place in the zodiac.
- **Usual orb** up to about 8 degrees, wider for the Sun and the Moon.
- **Symbol** a small circle with a line rising from it: ☌.
- **Key words** blending, focus, intensity, a new start.

## What a conjunction is

An aspect is an angle between two points in a chart. The conjunction is the simplest: two planets sit side by side, usually in the same sign. Astrologers read it as a fusion. The two planets act as one, each taking on the flavour of the other.

A conjunction is neither good nor bad in itself. Its tone depends on the planets involved. Venus with Jupiter is famously sweet. Mars with Saturn can feel like pressing the gas and the brake at once. Either way, a conjunction gives a chart a strong focus point where a lot of energy gathers.

## The orb

Planets seldom line up to the exact degree. Astrologers allow a range, called the orb, within which an aspect still counts. For a conjunction most allow about 8 degrees, and up to 10 when the Sun or the Moon takes part. The tighter the orb, the stronger the effect is said to be.

## Reading a conjunction

A simple way to read a conjunction is to put the key words of each planet together:

- **Sun with Moon** will and feeling united. Often born near a new Moon.
- **Sun with Mercury** thinking tied closely to identity. Very common.
- **Venus with Mars** love and desire joined. Warm and passionate.
- **Moon with Saturn** serious feelings and a strong sense of duty.
- **Mercury with Jupiter** a big, hopeful mind that loves to learn.
- **Mars with Pluto** great willpower and intensity.

Then add the sign and the house. A Venus and Mars conjunction in Libra in the seventh house points all that passion toward partnership.

## Stelliums

When three or more planets gather in one sign or house, astrologers call it a stellium. A stellium puts heavy weight on that sign or area of life. Someone with Sun, Mercury and Venus all in Capricorn may feel more Capricorn than the Sun alone suggests.

## Conjunctions in time

Conjunctions also mark cycles. Every new Moon is a conjunction of the Sun and the Moon, the start of a new lunar month. Jupiter and Saturn meet about every twenty years in a great conjunction that astrologers have watched since ancient times. The most recent one, on December 21, 2020, brought the two giants so close that they looked like a single bright star.

## Conjunctions in the real sky

A conjunction in the zodiac is often a lovely sight. When two bright planets meet in the evening sky, or the crescent Moon passes Venus, the pair glows side by side. These meetings are a good reason to look up. The Sky Altar app shows where each planet sits, so you can spot coming conjunctions over your camera.

## A gentle reminder

Astrology is a cultural tradition, not a science. A conjunction is a way to think about two parts of yourself working together. It does not predict events.
