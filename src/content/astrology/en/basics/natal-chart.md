## A map of one moment in the sky

A natal chart, or birth chart, is a map of the sky drawn for the exact time and place of a birth. Astrologers picture the sky as a wheel around the Earth. The wheel shows where the Sun, the Moon and the planets stood against the twelve signs of the zodiac, and how they sat above or below the horizon at that moment.

No two charts are quite the same. The Moon moves about one sign every two and a half days, and the rising sign changes about every two hours. Twins born a few minutes apart can even have slightly different charts. That is why astrologers ask for the birth time as well as the date and place.

## The four building blocks

- **Planets** the actors. Each stands for a part of life or of the self: the Sun for identity, the Moon for feelings, Venus for love, Mars for drive.
- **Signs** the style. A planet in a sign takes on that sign's colour, so Mars in Taurus acts slowly and steadily while Mars in Gemini acts with words.
- **Houses** the stage. The twelve houses divide the chart into areas of life such as home, work, partners and friends.
- **Aspects** the conversations. The angles between planets show which parts of the chart support or challenge each other.

## The angles of the chart

Four points frame each chart. The Ascendant, or rising sign, is the sign on the eastern horizon at birth. Opposite it sits the Descendant, linked with partners. The Midheaven is the highest point of the zodiac above you, tied to career and public life. Opposite it lies the lowest point, linked with home and roots. These points move fast, so they depend most on an exact birth time.

## The system Sky Altar uses

There are several ways to draw a chart. These articles and the Sky Altar app follow the most common Western approach:

- **Tropical zodiac** the signs start at the March equinox, so Aries 0 degrees is the point where the Sun crosses the celestial equator each spring.
- **Whole sign houses** the oldest house system: the rising sign is the first house, the next sign the second, and so on. Placidus, which divides the sky by time, is a popular modern alternative.
- **Major aspects** the conjunction, sextile, square, trine and opposition, the angles astrologers have used since ancient Greece.

Indian Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, tied to the fixed stars, and its signs fall about 24 degrees earlier. Neither choice is more correct as a tradition. They are two different ways to tell the story.

## How to read a chart, step by step

1. Start with the big three: the [Sun sign](../../basics/sun-sign/), the [Moon sign](../../basics/moon-sign/) and the [rising sign](../../basics/rising-sign/).
2. Look at which element and modality appear most, to sense the chart's overall tone.
3. Read each planet by its sign and house, one at a time.
4. Note the tight aspects, which tie planets together into themes.
5. Put the themes together into a story, and keep it kind and open.

## A tradition for reflection

Astrology is a cultural practice with roots in Babylon, Egypt, Greece, India and the Islamic world. It is not a science, and a chart cannot predict events, health or money. Many people still enjoy it as a mirror: a language for talking about character, timing and relationships. Read your chart in that spirit, as a set of questions about yourself rather than a verdict.
